<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-intro">Intro</h3>



<p>The introduction of the UK Consumer Composite Investments (CCI) framework via PS25/20 represents a step-change in the design and delivery of fund disclosures. Gone are the rigid, regulatory-prescribed formats of old, in favour of a more flexible approach enabling asset managers, insurers and other in-scope product manufacturers to better tailor information delivery to end investors.</p>



<p>While the need to test fund communications under the UK Consumer Duty is not new, this topic has certainly come to the fore in the context of CCI and in-scope firms will need to engage in a consumer testing cycle within their implementation projects to align with the Duty&#8217;s principles. A level of focus and urgency is needed to avoid rushed implementation later in 2026.</p>



<p>Confluence<sup>®</sup>&nbsp;is actively progressing here to support our clients in their consumer testing requirements, building on our initial design-focused phase for the Product Summary conducted in 2025.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-what-should-testing-achieve">What should testing achieve?</h3>



<p>Fundamentally, we&#8217;d boil the goals of testing down to three key outcomes:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Acceptability</strong> &#8211; Communications should enable consumers to make well-informed investment decisions via a sufficient level of understanding of the features, benefits and risks of a product.</li>



<li><strong>Accessibility</strong> &#8211; Creating a technically accessible document is one thing, but ensuring it actually meets the needs of a diverse potential investor base is another thing entirely. Communications need to adequately consider characteristics such as vulnerability.</li>



<li><strong>Defensibility</strong> &#8211; Building a sufficient evidence base through testing is critical to demonstrating alignment with the principles of the Consumer Duty &#8211; focused on the &#8216;consumer understanding&#8217; principle.</li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-testing-approaches">Testing approaches</h3>



<p>Testing approaches will vary depending on the specific use case and outcomes sought. Broadly speaking, testing falls under either a quantitative or qualitative approach, and tests may be used individually or in combination.</p>



<p><strong>Quantitative</strong></p>



<p>Quantitative testing serves as a &#8216;broader-brush&#8217; approach, aimed at targeting a wider pool of participants (e.g. N = 100+) via concise, online surveys. The intention here is to obtain fast, actionable insights by evaluating strengths and weaknesses in comprehension. Typically, feedback is achieved via a combination of highlighting tools for markup, and direct questions.</p>



<p>Evaluation may assess:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Stated and revealed understanding</li>



<li>What stands out and engages the reader</li>



<li>The navigability of the disclosure format</li>



<li>Likes, dislikes and preferences</li>



<li>Awareness and understanding of risk</li>



<li>Intended actions based on the information supplied &#8211; linking insights with actions and outcomes</li>
</ul>



<p>Testing might focus on a single Product Summary example, or it might involve applying an A:B testing approach comparing two documents, for example two versions of the same Product Summary.</p>



<p>This form of testing is intended to provide a directional understanding of communication performance against key evaluation criteria, with scoring and evidential reporting.</p>



<p><strong>Qualitative</strong></p>



<p>Qualitative testing involves a more in-depth approach, typically conducted via lengthier 1:1 interviews and/or digital panels with a smaller pool of participants. While the evaluation criteria above are all relevant, this approach offers an opportunity to solicit deeper insights and diagnostics on consumer understanding.</p>



<p>This form of testing may be more appropriate where multiple communications need to be tested as part of a consumer journey, or to reflect a range of nuances in a single communication type. For example, firms might wish to test sample narratives and communication approaches in more depth across different fund ranges, each with their own characteristics.</p>



<p>Equally, this more detailed form of testing might involve creating tasks for participants to complete to evaluate their success in navigating, understanding, and taking action based on the information communicated.</p>



<p>From an outcomes perspective, this will garner greater depth in diagnostics and recommendations, with more bespoke reporting and evidencing across a range of communications.</p>
